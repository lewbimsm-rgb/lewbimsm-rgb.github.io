## What matters when choosing pickleball accessories

Each item here solves one problem. So the first question is which problem you have. Sweaty or stiff hands point to an overgrip. A red neck after morning doubles points to a hat with a flap and a pair of sleeves. A back that hurts after picking up fifty balls points to a tube or a chair.

The eyes deserve their own paragraph. A 2025 study in JAMA Ophthalmology counted about 1,262 pickleball eye injuries treated in U.S. emergency rooms in 2024. Across the study, roughly 70 percent of cases were in people 50 or older. The American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends eyewear that meets the ASTM F3134 standard for racket sports. Regular eyeglasses do not count. Source: the [Review of Optometry summary](https://www.reviewofoptometry.com/news/article/surge-in-pickleball-correlated-with-increased-rate-of-eye-injury).

Braces are the one category where more is not better. An elbow strap or a knee sleeve can help a specific, diagnosed ache. It cannot fix a paddle that is too heavy or a grip that is too tight. It should not become a reason to play through pain, either. If a joint hurts for more than two weeks, swells, or changes your swing, see a physical therapist first.

### 1. Cushioned overgrip

This is for a hand that slides on the handle by the third game. It also suits arthritic hands that find a thin, hard grip painful. A cushioned overgrip wraps over the stock grip, soaks up sweat and adds a little padding. Tourna Grip feels dry and chalky; Wilson Pro Overgrip feels tacky. Both cost a few dollars and take two minutes to wrap. One wrap adds roughly a sixteenth of an inch, so a 4.25-inch handle still fits most hands.

```buyif
+ your hand slides on the handle by game three.
+ a thin, hard handle hurts an arthritic hand.
- your grip already feels thick for your hand.
- you will not rewrap it (a worn overgrip is worse than none).
```

This is for anyone who plays two hours outdoors and forgets to reapply. Sleeves rated UPF 50 block most UV without lotion. They feel cooler than bare skin once damp, and they save your forearm when you dive for a ball. Any lightweight pair in your size works; the pricey ones block no more sun.


This is for a specific, named ache, not general insurance. A counterforce elbow strap sits just below the elbow. It can take some load off a sore tendon on the outside of the arm. ## What to skip

A few pickleball accessories cost money and fix nothing. Lead tape for a first paddle. It adds weight to a paddle that is already heavy enough and makes a sore elbow worse. Bulky hinged knee braces without a diagnosis; they change how you move and hide a problem a therapist should see. Pickleball-branded towels, cooling towels and grip sprays at three times the price of the plain version. And any sunglasses sold without a lens material listed, because you cannot tell what they protect against.

## How we chose

These picks rest on published specs, fit data and player feedback. We have not tested them hands-on, and we are adding our own notes as the season goes on. We favored pickleball accessories that solve one problem for a player over 50. Most cost under about $30 and come in enough sizes to fit. We kept out anything with its own guide on this site.

```faq
Q: What pickleball accessories should a beginner buy first?
A: A cushioned overgrip, because it fixes the most common complaint, a slipping or stinging handle, for a few dollars. Protective sunglasses come next if you play doubles. Add a neck-flap hat if you play outdoors in the middle of the day.

Q: Do I need special glasses for pickleball?
A: Yes, if you want real protection. Regular eyeglasses and drugstore sunglasses are not rated for impact. Look for polycarbonate lenses that wrap the eye and a frame rated for racket sports. The American Academy of Ophthalmology points to the ASTM F3134 standard.

Q: Does an overgrip change my grip size?
A: A little. One cushioned wrap adds roughly a sixteenth of an inch to the grip circumference. For most hands that makes a 4.25-inch handle feel better. If your grip is already on the large side, choose a thin overgrip instead.

Q: Should I wear a knee brace or elbow strap to prevent injury?
A: Not as prevention. A strap or sleeve can help a named, diagnosed ache, and it can also hide a problem that needs a physical therapist. Court shoes, a warm-up and a lighter paddle prevent more injuries than any brace.
```
