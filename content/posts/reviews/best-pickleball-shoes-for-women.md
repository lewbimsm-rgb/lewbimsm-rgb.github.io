## Why running shoes roll ankles

Running shoes do one thing well: they move you forward. The sole is tall, soft and curved, and the upper is light mesh. That is the opposite of what pickleball asks for. Most of the game is a short shuffle to the side, a lunge, and a hard stop at the kitchen line.

When you push sideways in a running shoe, the tall sole tips and the mesh lets your foot slide over the edge. That is the classic rolled ankle. A court shoe fixes it with a flatter sole and a firm wall along the side of the foot. It also has a wider base under the heel. The [American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons](https://www.orthoinfo.org/staying-healthy/athletic-shoes/) puts it plainly. Court shoes give stability in all directions, and running shoes do not.

Tennis shoes count as court shoes. So do volleyball shoes for indoor play and the newer pickleball-specific models. Walking shoes, trail shoes and cross-trainers do not.

## What to look for in the best pickleball shoes for women

**Side-to-side (lateral) support.** Press the side of the shoe with your thumb. You want a firm wall, not soft mesh. Many court shoes add a small lip of rubber outside the midsole, called an outrigger. It widens the base when you lunge.

**A flat, grippy outsole.** Look for a zigzag (herringbone) pattern that sits flat on the ground. For outdoor courts, choose a harder rubber that resists wear. For an indoor wood floor, a softer gum rubber grips better but wears fast outside.

**Cushion under the heel.** Your knees take the stops. Look for a visible gel pad or a thick foam layer under the heel, with firmer foam under the arch so the shoe does not wobble.

**Width options.** Women's shoes come in B (standard), D (wide) and sometimes 2E (extra wide). If you have a bunion, a wide forefoot or feet that swell, a D width changes everything. New Balance offers the most widths; ASICS offers some.

**Weight.** Lighter feels quicker, but very light shoes often give up the side wall. For most women over 50, a mid-weight shoe is the safer buy.

**A toe cap.** If you drag your back toe on serves and lunges, get a rubber wrap over the toe. It keeps the shoe from wearing through in a month.

## How to try them on

Shop late in the day. Feet swell as the day goes on, and a shoe that fits at 9 a.m. can pinch by 4 p.m. [OrthoInfo](https://www.orthoinfo.org/staying-healthy/athletic-shoes/) suggests about a half inch of space between your longest toe and the front of the shoe. Leave enough room to wiggle your toes.

Bring your court socks, which are thicker than everyday socks. If you wear orthotics, bring those too, and pull out the shoe's own insole before you put them in. Then shuffle sideways a few steps in the store and stop hard. Your foot should not slide to the edge, and your heel should not lift.

## What to skip

**Running and walking shoes.** The sole is too tall and too soft for sideways moves. This is the most common cause of the rolled ankles we see at open play.

**Shoes with a rounded sole.** Some "comfort" sneakers have a rocker shape that rolls you forward. It feels great on a walk and terrible on a hard stop.

**Buying by size alone.** A 9 in ASICS, a 9 in New Balance and a 9 in Nike are three different shapes. Try the shoe on late in the day, or order two sizes and send one back.

**A gum sole outdoors.** Indoor shoes like the Gel-Rocket grip wonderfully inside and wear through quickly on concrete. Match the sole to where you play most.

## How we chose the best pickleball shoes for women

We did not run a lab test or weigh these shoes. The picks rest on the makers' published specs and width charts, and on what players report about fit and durability. We also watch what works on the feet of women at our local courts. We will add our own on-court notes and photos as we wear them.

```faq
Q: Can I play pickleball in running shoes?
A: You can, but you should not for long. Running shoes have a tall, soft, curved sole built for forward motion. Pickleball is mostly side-to-side, and that sole tips when you push sideways. A court shoe has a flatter sole and a firm side wall that holds your foot.

Q: Are tennis shoes the same as pickleball shoes?
A: Close enough for most players. Both have a flat outsole, a firm side wall and cushion for hard stops. Pickleball-specific shoes, like Selkirk's CourtStrike line, tune the fit and grip for the smaller court. A good tennis shoe from ASICS, New Balance or Nike works fine.

Q: What are the best pickleball shoes for women with wide feet?
A: Start with New Balance, which sells its 796 and FuelCell 996 lines in a wide width. Women's wide is a D; extra wide is 2E. Some ASICS Gel-Resolution versions also come in wide. Nike, adidas and On run standard, so try those on first.

Q: How often should I replace pickleball shoes?
A: Look at the outsole, not the calendar. When the zigzag tread under the ball of your foot goes smooth, or the heel leans to one side when the shoe sits on a table, replace it. Outdoor players often get a few months per pair.
```
