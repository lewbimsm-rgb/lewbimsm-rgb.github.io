Two Septembers ago I skipped sunscreen on a cool, cloudy morning and came home with ears the color of boiled shrimp. The next morning one of the women I play with handed me a tube from her bag without a word, which is how pickleball skincare really gets taught at a public court. Here is the routine I landed on, with the reasons, because I do better when I know why.

```quick
**The short version:** the one piece of pickleball skincare that truly matters is the sunscreen. Broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher on your face, neck, ears, arms and the backs of your hands about 15 minutes before you leave, then one more coat at the two-hour mark. A hat, sunglasses and an SPF lip balm ride along. The face pick is **EltaMD UV Clear SPF 46**; for arms and legs, **Supergoop PLAY Everyday Lotion SPF 50**. Afterward, a rinse, a gentle cleanser and a plain moisturizer, and a dermatologist once a year.
```

## The pickleball skincare routine

The bottle should say broad spectrum, SPF 30 or higher, and water resistant, which is the [American Academy of Dermatology's](https://www.aad.org/media/stats-sunscreen) standing advice. Put it on about 15 minutes before you leave and use more than feels polite; a shot glass covers an adult body. We all forget the ears, the back of the neck and the backs of the hands.

Go back at the two-hour mark, sooner if you have sweated hard or toweled off. I tie mine to the water: when I refill the [bottle](/best-water-bottles-for-pickleball/), the tube comes out too. And start each season with a fresh tube; the one that spent August in a hot car has had a harder summer than you did.

The rest is what sunscreen cannot do alone: a hat with a brim all the way around, because a visor leaves the ears and neck out in the open; UPF 50 sun sleeves, which are somehow cooler than bare skin once damp and spare you the arm reapply; sunglasses; and an SPF lip balm, because lips burn too and nobody, me included, remembers them.

## Mineral or chemical

This is the one real pickleball skincare debate. Mineral sunscreens, the zinc and titanium kind, rarely sting, so they suit sensitive skin and eyes that burn when you sweat; the trade is a thicker feel and sometimes a white cast. Chemical sunscreens rub in clear and feel lighter. The AAD says both work if you use enough and go back at two hours, so buy whichever you will actually wear.

## After you play

The after part of pickleball skincare is short, which is the good news: a rinse, a gentle cleanser and a plain moisturizer. CeraVe Moisturizing Cream if your skin runs dry, Vanicream Daily Facial Moisturizer if your face reacts to everything. At our age skin wants less, not more.

## The once-a-year appointment

Skin thins as we get older, heals more slowly, and keeps a record of every summer before anyone said the word sunscreen, so new spots turn up now. A prescription retinoid adds sun sensitivity, so the hat matters even more. One in five Americans develops skin cancer by 70, per the [Skin Cancer Foundation](https://www.skincancer.org/skin-cancer-information/skin-cancer-facts/), which advises a monthly look in the mirror and a yearly exam, sooner for anything new, changing or bleeding, or a sore that will not heal in a few weeks.

```faq
Q: What SPF do I need for pickleball?
A: SPF 30 or higher, broad spectrum and water resistant, which is the American Academy of Dermatology's advice. Higher numbers add a little, not a lot. A full amount, and a second coat at two hours, does more than the number on the bottle.

Q: How often should a pickleball player see a dermatologist?
A: Once a year for a full skin exam, with a monthly look-over at home in between, which is the Skin Cancer Foundation's guidance. Go sooner for any spot that is new, changing, bleeding, or not healing in a few weeks.
```
