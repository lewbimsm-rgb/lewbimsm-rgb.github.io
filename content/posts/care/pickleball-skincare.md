Pickleball skincare comes down to one routine: broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher on every exposed inch before you leave, a reapply at two hours, a hat and sun sleeves, and a rinse and moisturizer after. ## The routine

Choose a sunscreen that says broad spectrum, SPF 30 or higher, and water resistant. That is the [American Academy of Dermatology's](https://www.aad.org/media/stats-sunscreen) standing advice. Put it on 15 minutes before you go out, and use enough: a shot glass covers an adult body. Cover the parts players forget: ears, back of the neck, backs of the hands.

Reapply at the two-hour mark, sooner if you have sweated hard or toweled off. Keep a fresh tube in the paddle bag each season; last summer's from the hot car is past its best.

Add an SPF lip balm, a hat with a brim all the way around (a visor leaves ears and neck out), sunglasses, and UPF 50 sun sleeves, which feel cooler than bare skin and save you the arm reapply.

## Mineral or chemical

Mineral sunscreens (zinc, titanium) rarely sting, so they suit sensitive skin and eyes that burn when you sweat, but they feel thicker and can leave a white cast. Chemical sunscreens rub in clear and feel lighter. The AAD says both work when you use enough and reapply. Nothing is sweatproof.

## Over 50: the yearly check

Skin thins with age, heals slower, and carries every summer you spent without sunscreen decades ago, which is why new spots show up now. A prescription retinoid adds sun sensitivity, so the hat matters even more.

The [Skin Cancer Foundation](https://www.skincancer.org/skin-cancer-information/skin-cancer-facts/) advises a monthly self-check; add a professional exam once a year, and book sooner for any spot that is new, changing or bleeding, or a sore that does not heal in a few weeks. That appointment matters more than any product here.

## FAQ

```faq
Q: What SPF do I need for pickleball?
A: SPF 30 or higher, broad spectrum, and water resistant, per the American Academy of Dermatology. Higher numbers add a little, not a lot. Using a full amount and reapplying every two hours matters more than the number on the bottle.

Q: Is mineral or chemical sunscreen better for pickleball?
A: Both work when you use enough. Mineral (zinc, titanium) suits sensitive skin and eyes that sting when you sweat. Chemical rubs in clear and feels lighter. Pick the one you will put on every session.

Q: How often should a pickleball player see a dermatologist?
A: Once a year for a full skin exam, plus a monthly self-check at home. That is the Skin Cancer Foundation's guidance. Go sooner for any spot that is new, changing, bleeding, or not healing in a few weeks.
```
