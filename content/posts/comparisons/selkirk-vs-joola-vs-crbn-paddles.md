Pick Selkirk if you want control, JOOLA if you came from tennis and still like to hit, and CRBN if the soft feel of raw carbon matters most to you. For most of us that is the whole Selkirk vs JOOLA vs CRBN decision, and Selkirk wins it more often than not because it sells a real paddle at every price, from an SLK under $100 to a flagship built in the USA. Here is why, and the one paddle from each brand I would point a friend to.

```quick
**The short answer:** Selkirk for control and range, JOOLA for power, CRBN for raw carbon feel. If this is your first real paddle, or your elbow has ever had opinions, start at 16 mm, 7.6 to 8.0 oz and a 4.25" grip, from any of the three.
```

## Selkirk vs JOOLA vs CRBN: the numbers that matter

Weight first: most paddles from all three brands land between 7.6 and 8.3 oz, and your arm pays for every ounce by the third game, so if your elbow has ever complained, stay at 8.0 oz or under. Core thickness next: 16 mm is the control core and 14 mm the power core, and the thicker one soaks up more of the ball's energy, so shots stay in and your elbow takes less shock. I would start at 16 mm unless you already know you want power.

Raw carbon faces (the SLK Halo, CRBN, JOOLA's pro lines) grip the ball for spin and feel soft, while the fiberglass on most under-$100 paddles pops harder and wears sooner. A 4.25" grip is standard at all three and fits most adults. Entry paddles run under $100, mid-range raw carbon about $100 to $200 and flagships $200 to $280, and because counterfeit pro paddles exist and a fake has no warranty, buy from the brand store or from an Amazon listing the brand itself sells.

## One paddle from each brand

### Selkirk: control at every price

Selkirk made its name on control paddles with a big sweet spot, and [Selkirk's paddle page](https://www.selkirk.com/collections/paddles) lists core, weight and grip for every model. The SLK line starts under $100, the Luxx Control Air is the plush one, with a 20 mm core that calms fast balls, and the Vanguard is built in Selkirk's own factory in Idaho.

The SLK Halo (Control) is the one to start with, because it pairs the 16 mm core and raw carbon face with a price about half of a flagship. The face grips the ball, so dinks and drops stay low without a perfect swing, and off-center hits still go in. It also comes in Power and Max versions, but Control is the one to buy first.

```buyif
+ this is your first real paddle and you want it to last past month one
+ you play doubles and want the ball to land in, not fly long
- you already hit hard and want more pop off the face
- you want to spend under $100
```

### CRBN: the soft one for sore elbows

CRBN (say "carbon") built its name on raw carbon fiber faces that feel soft and grippy, which is why control players love it. The X series comes in 1X elongated, 2X widebody and 3X hybrid, in 14 mm or 16 mm, and the TruFoam Genesis at [CRBN's store](https://crbnpickleball.com/) swaps the honeycomb core for full foam at 14 mm, the newest and priciest of the three.

For tennis elbow or arthritis, the 2X in 16 mm is the pick, because a wide face has the biggest sweet spot, so off-center hits still feel soft, and the thick core takes the sting out of hard balls before it reaches your elbow. The raw carbon adds spin without extra swing effort, so a short, relaxed motion still gets you a good dink.

```cta
Search Amazon for the CRBN 2X | https://www.amazon.com/s?k=crbn+2x+pickleball+paddle&tag=smartselec0a7-20 | Amazon
Pick the 16 mm version, not the 14 mm, if your elbow is the reason you are here.
```

### JOOLA: power for the big swingers

JOOLA is the power brand, and its pro paddles carry the name of Ben Johns, the best-known men's pro in the sport. The Perseus is the lighter, faster line and the Hyperion the head-heavy one, and JOOLA refreshes them about once a year with a new suffix (the current ones say Pro IV), so shop by line name at [JOOLA's store](https://www.joola.com/).

Former tennis players swing big and hit flat, and the Perseus in 16 mm rewards that with an elongated shape for reach and a carbon face that adds spin on drives, while the thicker core gives some control back. The Hyperion hits harder still, but Ben Johns can swing a 14 mm head-heavy paddle for five hours and most of us cannot, so buy for your arm, not his.


If $200 is too much for a first season, JOOLA's Essentials and Selkirk's entry SLK paddles are real brands for under $100, and I would not go cheaper, because no-name sets often have thin cores and grips that come apart. A used flagship is a false bargain too, since cores go soft after a year of hard play and dead spots do not show in photos.

I have not played all three lines myself; this Selkirk vs JOOLA vs CRBN comparison comes from the brands' spec pages and from what the players at my courts say after a season.

## Common questions

```faq
Q: Selkirk vs JOOLA vs CRBN: which is best for a beginner?
A: Selkirk, for most of us. The SLK Halo gives you a 16 mm core and a raw carbon face at about half the price of a flagship, and CRBN is close behind if you want the softest feel. JOOLA's pro lines are power paddles, so new players tend to hit long.

Q: Should I get a 14 mm or a 16 mm paddle?
A: 16 mm, unless you already know you want power. The thicker core keeps the ball in, gives a bigger sweet spot and sends less shock to your elbow. 14 mm hits harder but needs a cleaner swing. All three brands offer their main lines in both.

Q: Which paddle is best for tennis elbow?
A: A widebody 16 mm paddle at 8.0 oz or under, with a 4.25" grip and a loose hold. The CRBN 2X and the Selkirk Luxx Control Air are the two to look at first. Lighter helps, but rest helps more, and if pain changes your swing, see a physical therapist.
```
