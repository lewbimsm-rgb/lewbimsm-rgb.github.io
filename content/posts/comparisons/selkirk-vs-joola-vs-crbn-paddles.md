## What each of the three brands does best

Each brand has a clear personality. That personality tells you more than any model name, because models change every year and the lines stay.

### Selkirk: control, choice and a USA-built flagship

Selkirk made its name on control paddles with a big sweet spot. The SLK line is the entry door. It starts under $100 and includes a raw carbon option, the SLK Halo. The Luxx Control Air line is the plush one, with a thick 20 mm core that calms fast balls. The Vanguard line sits at the top, and Selkirk builds it in its own factory in Idaho. [Selkirk's paddle page](https://www.selkirk.com/collections/paddles) lists core, weight and grip for every model, so the brand is easy to shop.

### JOOLA: power, spin and the Ben Johns paddles

JOOLA is the power brand. Its pro paddles carry the name of Ben Johns, the best-known men's pro in the sport. The Perseus line is the lighter, faster one. The Hyperion line carries more weight in the head and hits harder. Both come in 14 mm and 16 mm. JOOLA refreshes them about once a year with a new suffix (the current ones say Pro IV), so shop by line name. The Essentials line is the under-$100 entry point at [JOOLA's store](https://www.joola.com/).

### CRBN: raw carbon feel and a foam-core first

CRBN (say "carbon") built its name on raw carbon fiber faces. The X series comes in three shapes: 1X elongated, 2X widebody and 3X hybrid, in 14 mm or 16 mm. The feel is soft and grippy, which is why control players love it. The TruFoam Genesis line swaps the usual honeycomb core for a full foam core at 14 mm. It is the newest tech of the three brands, and the priciest. Both lines live at [CRBN's store](https://crbnpickleball.com/).

## Selkirk vs JOOLA vs CRBN: the specs that matter

**Weight.** Most paddles from all three brands land between 7.6 and 8.3 oz. If you are over 50, or your elbow has ever complained, stay at 8.0 oz or under. A heavier paddle hits harder, but your arm pays for it by the third game.

**Core thickness.** 16 mm is the control core, and 14 mm is the power core. A thicker core soaks up more of the ball's energy. Your shots stay in and your elbow takes less shock. Start with 16 mm unless you already know you want power.

**Face material.** Raw carbon (the SLK Halo, CRBN, JOOLA's pro lines) grips the ball for spin and feels soft. Fiberglass (most under-$100 paddles) pops the ball harder and feels livelier, but it wears sooner.

### Grip, shape, price and warranty

**Grip size.** 4.25" fits most adults, and all three brands treat it as the standard. A quick test: hold the paddle and slide a finger of your other hand between your fingertips and your palm. It should just fit.

**Shape.** Widebody paddles give you the biggest sweet spot and the most forgiveness. Elongated paddles add reach and power but shrink the sweet spot. Standard shapes sit in between.

**Price band.** Entry paddles run under $100 (Selkirk SLK, JOOLA Essentials). Mid-range raw carbon runs about $100 to $200. Flagships run $200 to $280 across all three brands.

**Warranty and where to buy.** Each brand covers manufacturing defects for a set period, and the terms differ by line. Read the warranty page before you check out. Buy from the brand store or from an Amazon listing that the brand itself sells. Counterfeit pro paddles exist, and a fake has no warranty at all.

If your elbow already hurts, a lighter 16 mm paddle helps, but it does not replace rest. The Mayo Clinic's page on [tennis elbow](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/tennis-elbow/symptoms-causes/syc-20351987) explains when to see someone.

## What "USA Pickleball approved" means

Every tournament sanctioned by [USA Pickleball](https://usapickleball.org/) requires a paddle from its approved list. The main lines from Selkirk, JOOLA and CRBN all carry that approval. For park play and league nights at the rec center, nobody checks. So the stamp matters only if you plan to enter a sanctioned event.

One wrinkle: a new generation can take a few weeks to appear on the list. If you have a tournament coming up, buy the model that already shows there.

## Mistakes people make

**Buying by pro name.** Ben Johns can swing a 14 mm head-heavy paddle for five hours. Most of us cannot. Buy for your arm, not his.

**Starting with 14 mm.** The power core feels exciting in the store and sends balls long on the court. Go 16 mm first.

**Ignoring the weight.** Anything over 8.3 oz is a workout. If you are over 50, read the spec line before the reviews.

**Buying a used flagship.** Cores go soft after a year of hard play, and dead spots do not show in photos. A new SLK Halo beats a tired Perseus.

## Common questions

```faq
Q: Selkirk vs JOOLA vs CRBN: which is best for a beginner?
A: Selkirk, for most people. The SLK Halo gives you a 16 mm core and a raw carbon face at about half the price of a flagship. CRBN is close behind if you want the softest feel. JOOLA's pro lines are power paddles, so new players tend to hit long.

Q: Should I get a 14 mm or a 16 mm paddle?
A: 16 mm, unless you already know you want power. The thicker core keeps the ball in, gives a bigger sweet spot and sends less shock to your elbow. 14 mm hits harder but needs a cleaner swing. All three brands offer their main lines in both.

Q: Which paddle is best for tennis elbow?
A: A widebody 16 mm paddle at 8.0 oz or under, with a 4.25" grip and a loose hold. The CRBN 2X and the Selkirk Luxx Control Air are the two to look at first. Lighter helps, but rest helps more. If pain changes your swing, see a physical therapist.

Q: Do I need a USA Pickleball approved paddle?
A: Only for sanctioned tournaments. The main lines from all three brands carry the approval, so you will have it anyway. At the park and in rec-center leagues, nobody checks. If you plan to compete, buy a model that already appears on the approved list.

Q: Should I buy from the brand store or from Amazon?
A: Either, as long as the seller is the brand itself. The brand store has every version and a simple warranty claim. Amazon is faster and often matches the price. Avoid third-party sellers on pro paddles, because counterfeits exist and carry no warranty.
```
