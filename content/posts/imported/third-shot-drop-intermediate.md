## Understand the job

After the serve and return, the serving team hits the third shot. A drop tries to land softly in the kitchen so the receiving team cannot attack it easily. It buys time to move forward. It is not a guaranteed invitation to sprint to the net. A high drop may require you to stop and defend the next ball instead.

## Start closer than the baseline

Practice drops from midcourt first. Use a compact swing, meet the ball in front, and aim for a soft descending flight. Move farther back as control improves. Think of the shot as lifting and guiding rather than chopping at the ball. There is no single perfect arc for every situation: wind, ball speed, and contact height all change the required touch.

## Earn your movement

After hitting, watch the opponent’s contact point. If your ball stays low, move forward together with your partner. If it sits high, stop in balance before the opponent swings. Advancing in stages is better than getting caught running through a hard reply. Communicate about who covers the middle, especially if only one player can move safely forward.

## A measurable drill

Have a partner return a gentle feed while you hit ten drops from midcourt. Record how many force a low contact rather than counting only kitchen bounces. Repeat from farther back. Finish with points that begin with a serve and return, and discuss whether your third shot created time to advance. A drive can also be appropriate; learn to choose based on the incoming ball rather than using a drop mechanically.
