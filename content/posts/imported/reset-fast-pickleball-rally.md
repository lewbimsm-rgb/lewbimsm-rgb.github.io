## Defense is a decision

When opponents drive at you, swinging harder back can turn a manageable rally into a scramble. A reset is a soft defensive reply designed to reduce their attacking opportunities. It may land in the kitchen or simply stay low enough to make the next shot less dangerous. Your immediate goal is neutralizing pressure, not winning from a compromised position.

## Keep the paddle stable

Prepare the paddle in front and minimize the backswing. Use the incoming pace rather than adding a large swing. Adjust grip pressure so the ball does not spring off uncontrollably, while maintaining a stable face. Very low balls may need to bounce. Avoid forcing a volley when stepping back and playing a bounce would give you more control.

## Train with predictable pace

Ask a partner to feed moderate drives toward your comfortable contact zone. Block five balls, then try five soft resets. Increase speed only after both players can maintain control. Safety glasses are worth considering during close-range practice. Never fire hard shots at an unprepared partner, and stop if fatigue starts to compromise reaction or technique.

## Read the next ball

A reset is useful only if you recover afterward. Reestablish balance, bring the paddle up, and watch whether the opponent contacts below or above net height. Do not celebrate a soft landing by standing still. Track whether your reset gave you time or produced another attackable ball; that feedback is more informative than labeling each attempt good or bad.
