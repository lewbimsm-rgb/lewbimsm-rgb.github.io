## Movement comes before the look

Choose clothing that allows a squat, a reach, and a side step without pulling or slipping. Waistbands should stay secure without restricting movement. Shorts, skirts, or dresses with comfortable liners can be convenient, but the best option is personal. Test the full range of movement at home instead of discovering a problem during a point.

## Fabric depends on conditions

Lightweight, moisture-managing fabrics can feel more comfortable in heat. Cotton is familiar and soft but can hold moisture. Seams and fabric texture may matter if you are prone to irritation. Check washing instructions; aggressive heat or fabric treatments can alter stretch and finish. Brand descriptions are helpful starting points, not guarantees of comfort.

## Sun coverage is part of the outfit

A hat, sunglasses suitable for activity, and covering garments can complement sunscreen. If you choose UPF-rated clothing, check the stated rating and care guidance. Loose, breathable coverage may be more practical than applying sunscreen repeatedly to every exposed area. In strong heat, clothing cannot substitute for shade, breaks, and a sensible playing schedule.

## Build a small rotation

Start with two comfortable outfits and suitable socks before buying a complete matching wardrobe. Note which pieces chafe, retain heat, or lose shape. Look at durability and care requirements alongside price. Retailer links on this site are there to help you explore categories, not to imply that an outfit improves your rating. No clothing item replaces footwork practice or suitable court shoes.
