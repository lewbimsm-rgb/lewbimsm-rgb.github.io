## What a dink actually does

A dink is a controlled shot that lands in the opponent’s kitchen. Its purpose is to make attacking difficult, not simply to keep the ball short. If your dink floats high, it may invite a put-away. Begin with a stable stance, paddle out in front, and a compact motion. Let the ball bounce when needed rather than reaching for a difficult volley.

## Use your legs and a quiet paddle

Bend through your knees and hips so your paddle can meet the ball comfortably. Avoid scooping with a large wrist flick. Guide the ball with a small swing and enough clearance to cross the net. A relaxed grip helps, but do not hold the paddle so loosely that its face twists on contact. The feeling should be controlled rather than delicate.

## A cooperative lesson

Stand opposite a partner at the kitchen line and aim to complete ten cooperative dinks. Start straight ahead, then try crosscourt, where the longer diagonal gives more space. Count consecutive balls that bounce in the kitchen, not rallies won. If control breaks down, move the target closer to the center. Spend five minutes on each direction and finish with a short game where only soft shots are allowed.

## Know when not to dink

A high ball may be attackable, while a low ball usually calls for patience. Do not force a dink from an uncomfortable position simply because you planned to play softly. Return to balance first. When following the ball sideways, use short steps and avoid crossing your feet in a way that delays recovery. Check the kitchen boundary before volleying, and remember that a bounced ball can be played from inside the kitchen.
