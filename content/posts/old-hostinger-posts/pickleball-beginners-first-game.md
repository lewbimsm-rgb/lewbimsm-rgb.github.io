## Start with the court, not the score

Pickleball uses a small court divided by a net. The seven-foot area on either side of the net is the non-volley zone, usually called the kitchen. You may enter it to hit a ball that has bounced, but you cannot volley while touching the zone or its boundary. Momentum matters too: stepping into the kitchen after a volley is a fault. Walk the lines before playing so you can recognize them without looking down during a rally.

## The two-bounce rule makes the rally

The serve must land in the diagonally opposite service court, beyond the kitchen. The receiving team must let it bounce before returning it. The serving team must then let that return bounce before hitting it. After those two bounces, either team can volley outside the kitchen. Beginners often lose points by charging forward immediately after serving; stay back until you have handled the return.

## Make scoring a conversation

In traditional doubles side-out scoring, only the serving team scores. Scores are called as serving-team points, receiving-team points, then server number. The opening server begins at 0–0–2. Most recreational games are played to 11, win by two, but formats vary. Ask your group which scoring system they use before starting. If you lose track, pause and confirm rather than guessing.

## Your first-session checklist

Bring court shoes, water, and a paddle you can swing comfortably. Warm up with easy movement, short volleys, and gentle groundstrokes. Spend your first game trying to clear the net with margin instead of aiming at the sidelines. Call the score before every serve and make line calls promptly. Ask an experienced player to explain one rule at a time; there is no prize for knowing everything on day one. For organized competition, consult the current USA Pickleball rulebook rather than relying on casual court advice.
