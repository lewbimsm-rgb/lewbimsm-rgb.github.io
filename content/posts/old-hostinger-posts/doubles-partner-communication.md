## Agree on a few words

Before playing, decide on simple calls such as “mine,” “yours,” and “switch.” Calls should come early enough to affect the shot. Shouting after your partner has already swung adds noise rather than clarity. Agree on who takes a comfortable middle ball, but treat that as a flexible plan. Forehand preference alone does not account for positioning, momentum, or reach.

## Move as a connected pair

When your partner moves wide, shift enough to reduce the open gap. When one player advances, the other should consider whether advancing is safe too. You do not need to stand at identical depths in every rally, but a large mismatch gives opponents angles. Observe the ball and your partner rather than staring only at the player across from you.

## Reset between points

A brief acknowledgment after a point can keep communication constructive. Discuss patterns between games rather than giving a technical lecture after every miss. Try “let’s return deeper” instead of “you always hit short.” If one player is targeted repeatedly, build a shared plan that improves coverage without making that player feel blamed.

## Practice a middle-ball game

Play a cooperative rally in which feeds alternate between the middle and a sideline. Call every middle ball before contact. Stop when both players swing or neither commits, then agree on what information was missing. Keep the pace low until the calls feel natural. The drill works best with supportive partners who value coordination over winning a practice rally.
