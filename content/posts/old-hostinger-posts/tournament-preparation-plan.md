## Know the event

Check the event’s scoring format, ball, surface, check-in requirements, and equipment rules. Confirm whether your paddle meets the organizer’s requirements. Read the current event guidance instead of assuming every tournament follows your local open-play format. Travel plans and start times affect preparation just as much as your favorite warm-up drill.

## Practice the situations you expect

Use training games that reflect likely match conditions. Practice serve returns under pressure, middle-ball communication, and defending from the transition zone. In doubles, agree on initial positioning and a simple plan for switching tactics. Do not attempt a complete technical rebuild in the final practice before competition; familiar patterns are usually easier to trust under pressure.

## Pack for the ordinary problems

Bring water, suitable food, spare grips, a towel, sun protection, and any permitted backup equipment. Break in shoes before event day. Plan recovery between matches without experimenting with unfamiliar supplements or large amounts of caffeine. Nutrition and hydration needs vary with the weather and the individual; medical conditions may require personalized guidance.

## Give every match a reset

After a match, record one useful tactical observation and one thing to keep doing. Allow time to cool down and review the next opponent if that information is available. Avoid turning every result into a judgment about your level. Competition provides feedback, but a single event does not define progress. For professional ambitions, work with a qualified coach on an ongoing competition and training plan.
