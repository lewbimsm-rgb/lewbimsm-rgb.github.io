```quick
If you are wondering how to serve in pickleball, start with a serve you can repeat: underhand, loose grip, aimed at the middle of the box, with no ambition beyond getting it in. Eight out of ten in before you add anything fancy.
```

If you are new to pickleball, this is the serve I wish someone had shown me on day one. It is not fast and it is not clever. It goes in, and that is the whole point: the serve only starts the rally, and you cannot win a point you never start.

## The two serves you are allowed to hit

**The volley serve** is the one most people use. You hit the ball out of the air, underhand, with the paddle moving upward and your contact point below your waist. Think of a gentle bowling motion.

**The drop serve** is the forgiving one. You let the ball drop from your hand (no tossing it up or pushing it down), let it bounce, and hit it however you like. If the volley serve feels awkward, start here. Nobody will think less of you, and plenty of good players use it.

Both have to land in the service box diagonally across from you, past the kitchen line. The [rules post](/pickleball-rules-for-beginners/) has the full list in plain words, and the [official rulebook](https://usapickleball.org/what-is-pickleball/official-rules/) has the fine print.

## How to serve in pickleball, in three steps

1. **Set up calm.** Stand a step behind the baseline, feet comfortable, ball in your free hand around waist height. Breathe out.
2. **Swing low to high.** Loose grip, like you are holding a bird. Let the paddle swing from your hip toward your target. Contact the ball out in front of you, not beside you.
3. **Finish toward the box.** Your paddle should end up pointing where you wanted the ball to go. Then step in and get ready.

That is it. No wrist flick, no spin, no leap. Those can come later, if ever.

## Aim big, not clever

Pick the middle of the diagonal box, well past the kitchen line, and aim there every time. A deep serve is nice, but a deep serve that goes long is just a lost point. Picture a big, friendly rectangle rather than a corner the size of a dinner plate. When eight out of ten land, you can start nudging your target deeper.

## The ten-minute routine

I do this with one paddle, a handful of balls and an empty court before open play.

- Ten serves to the left box. Count how many land.
- Ten to the right box. Count again.
- Ten aimed a little deeper than you think you should.
- Last ten: say the score out loud before each one, like a real game.

If fewer than eight out of ten are landing, slow the swing down and simplify. The goal of the drill is legal serves, not pretty ones.

## Three mistakes I see every week

- **Trying to hit it hard.** Pace comes from a relaxed swing, not from muscle. Hard serves go long.
- **Letting the ball get away from you.** If you drop or release the ball too far from your body, you end up reaching and chopping at it. Keep it close.
- **Rebuilding the serve after one miss.** Everyone misses. Notice whether your misses are mostly long, short or wide, and change one thing at your next practice, not mid-game.

> **SmartSelect Tip:** Before every serve, do the same two things in the same order. Mine are "bounce the ball twice, breathe out." A small ritual tells your body it is time to repeat the thing it already knows.

```faq
Q: Can I serve overhand in pickleball?
A: No. The volley serve must be underhand with contact below the waist, and the drop serve must bounce first. Anything that looks like a tennis serve is a fault.
Q: Do I have to call the score before I serve?
A: Yes, in rec play and in tournaments. Say it out loud, wait until the receiver is ready, then serve. It also gives you a calm second to breathe.
Q: Is the drop serve really allowed?
A: Yes, it has been legal since 2021. Let the ball fall from your hand without throwing it down, let it bounce, and hit it any way you like.
```
