## Sun protection is the first priority

For outdoor play, choose a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher. If you expect sweat, look for a water-resistance claim and follow its stated duration and reapplication directions. Many Korean daily sunscreens prioritize light texture but are not intended for sustained sweating. A pleasing finish is not a substitute for an appropriate product label.

## Apply before the first rally

Follow the product’s directions for timing and amount, covering commonly missed areas such as ears and the back of the neck. Reapply as directed, including after heavy sweating or toweling. Sunscreen works alongside shade, hats, and protective clothing. Do not assume a tinted product or makeup with SPF gives the same coverage as properly applied dedicated sunscreen.

## A gentle post-court routine

After playing, cleanse gently to remove sweat and sunscreen, then use a moisturizer suited to your skin. A complicated sequence of exfoliants and active ingredients is not necessary after every session. If your face is irritated, avoid introducing multiple new products together. Patch testing can help identify sensitivity, but it cannot guarantee a reaction will never occur.

## Read local labels

Product names and formulations can differ between markets. Verify the exact packaging, local compliance, expiration, and the seller’s authenticity policies before purchasing. We do not make medical claims about skincare or promise acne treatment. If irritation persists or you have a diagnosed skin condition, consult a dermatologist. This guide is general education, not a personalized skincare prescription.
