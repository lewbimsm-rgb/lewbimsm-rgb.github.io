## Get ready before the opponent hits

Begin with your feet comfortably apart and your paddle in front. As the opponent prepares to strike, use a small split step or light settling movement so you are not trapped on one foot. The goal is readiness, not a dramatic jump. Stay low enough to move but relaxed enough to react. Practice without a ball before combining the movement with a rally.

## Move, then strike

Running through contact often sends the ball somewhere unintended. Move early, slow down near the ball, and establish balance whenever time allows. Use short adjustment steps for nearby balls. For wider balls, turn and move rather than stretching beyond control. Do not backpedal blindly for a lob; turn safely, communicate with your partner, and let an unreachable ball go.

## A safe shadow drill

Mark three imaginary positions along the kitchen line. Move from center to one side, return to center, and repeat on the other side at easy speed. Keep the paddle ready and stop in balance at each position. Try three short rounds with a rest between them. Increase pace only if your footing remains secure. Clear loose balls off the court before doing any movement drill.

## Footwear is part of the lesson

Court shoes designed for lateral movement are generally a more appropriate starting point than soft running shoes. Fit, traction, and comfort matter more than a brand name. A worn outsole can be a reason to replace shoes even if the upper still looks fine. If you have ongoing pain, dizziness, or an injury history, ask a qualified health professional about suitable activity before pushing harder.
