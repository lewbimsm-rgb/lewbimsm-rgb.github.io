Last month I got to the courts at eight, unzipped my bag, and found two cracked balls, a hat I don't even like, and no sunscreen. My doubles partner lent me a ball. The sun did what the sun does. I drove home with a pink nose and a list. So here is what to pack in your pickleball bag, learned the hard way, in the order it should go in.

```quick
**The short version:** paddle, three outdoor balls, court shoes, a water bottle and sunscreen go in first. Then a towel, a hat, sunglasses, sun sleeves, an overgrip and a snack. Last come the small things you only miss when you need them: blister pads, an instant ice pack, electrolyte packets and a light jacket. Pack it once, and most of it simply lives there.
```

## The five to pack in your pickleball bag first

![Pickleball bag checklist graphic with the 15 things to pack in your pickleball bag in three columns: must-haves, comfort items and glad-I-had-it extras](/images/what-to-pack-in-your-pickleball-bag-card.png)

**A paddle, obviously.** If you are still choosing, a 16 mm paddle somewhere around 7.6 to 8 ounces with a 4.25-inch grip is the safe first buy, and you do not need to spend more than about a hundred dollars to get one you will like. I walk through the three big brands in the [paddle comparison](/selkirk-vs-joola-vs-crbn-paddles/) if you want the longer version.

**Three outdoor balls, not thirty.** Outdoor balls are harder than the indoor kind, with smaller holes, and they crack when the morning is cold, which is why you want spares. Three is plenty. Franklin X-40 is the ball at most parks, and it is on [USA Pickleball's](https://usapickleball.org/) approved list, so you are practicing with what everyone else is hitting.

**Court shoes, which are a different animal from running shoes.** A running shoe is built to go forward. Pickleball is mostly sideways, and a sideways lunge in a forward shoe is how ankles get rolled. A court shoe has a flat, grippy sole and some support at the sides. I keep mine in the bag so the soles stay clean for the court. There are guides for [women](/best-pickleball-shoes-for-women/) and [men](/best-pickleball-shoes-for-men/) if you want names.

**A water bottle you can open with one hand.** The other hand has a paddle in it. An insulated steel bottle, 24 to 32 ounces, with a lid that does not leak in the bag, and a straw lid if you like to sip between points. The [water bottle guide](/best-water-bottles-for-pickleball/) has the ones I see on the benches.

**Sunscreen, in a stick.** SPF 30 or higher, broad spectrum, on the face, neck, ears, arms and the backs of the hands, with a second coat at the two-hour mark. I switched to a stick after a bottle leaked onto my paddle face, which is a particular kind of sticky you do not forget. The [skincare post](/pickleball-skincare/) covers the rest.

## The six that make the morning nicer

A **small microfiber towel** for your hands and the handle. A sweaty grip becomes a tense grip, and a tense grip becomes a sore elbow, so the towel is really elbow insurance.

A **hat or visor**. The visor lets heat out the top of your head; the brimmed hat covers the ears and the back of the neck, which my dermatologist cares about more than I used to. Either one from the bin at the sporting goods store is fine.

**Sunglasses that could take a hit.** Polarized lenses cut the glare off a bright court, but the real reason is the ball. At the kitchen line it comes at your face faster than you would think, and wrap-around polycarbonate lenses are the difference between a flinch and a bad afternoon. Drugstore lenses are not impact rated.

**Sun sleeves**, the UPF 50 kind. No reapplying on the arms, they feel cooler than bare skin once they are damp, and they have saved my forearm in more than one dive I should not have attempted.

**One spare overgrip.** When the old one goes slick, you will want it. Tourna feels dry and chalky; Wilson feels tacky. A three-pack lasts a summer.

**A snack.** A banana, a nut bar, a sleeve of crackers. Two hours of doubles burns more than you expect, and the shaky, cranky third game is usually hunger rather than age. I say this as someone who blamed age for a year.

## The four you will only miss once

**Blister pads.** The hydrocolloid kind, plus a couple of regular bandages and a roll of athletic tape, in a sandwich bag you refill when you use one.

**An instant ice pack.** The squeeze-to-start kind, for the rolled ankle or the knee that suddenly has opinions. Cold does the most good in the first few minutes, so keep it somewhere you can reach without unpacking. Buy the two-pack at the pharmacy and keep both.

**Electrolyte packets.** Water is fine for an hour. For two hours in heat, or if you cramp at night, one packet in the second bottle helps more than it should. Nuun tablets are the inexpensive version; Liquid I.V. and LMNT are the fancier ones.

**A light jacket.** A packable windbreaker or a quarter-zip for the cool first game and the walk back to the car. The one you already own is the right one.

And a small bonus: slide the paddle into a **neoprene sleeve** or the cover it came in. Bag zippers and hot trunks are hard on a paddle face.

## What I finally took out

For a whole season I carried a beach towel, a glass bottle, a second paddle I never once used, and a knee brace nobody had asked me to wear. The bag weighed what a toddler weighs. These days the beach towel is a hand towel, the glass is steel, the spare paddle stays home, and the brace waits until a physical therapist says otherwise. The heavy sunscreen bottle went too, for reasons described above.

## And the bag itself

Start with how you get to the court. If you park beside it and carry one paddle, a sling is plenty; it sits across one shoulder and swings to the front so you can reach a pocket without taking it off. If you walk a long way, or you carry shoes, a jacket and two bottles, a backpack is kinder to a stiff neck.

Then look for four things. A fence hook, so the bag hangs at eye level instead of sitting on a wet court. A shoe pocket, so clean clothes stay away from the soles. An insulated pocket, which keeps a bottle cold and gives the ice pack a home. And a padded spot for your phone, because a phone under a pile of balls has a short life. Color matters less than the zippers; big pulls you can grab with sweaty hands are the thing you notice every single time.

```faq
Q: How many pickleballs should I pack?
A: Two or three. Outdoor balls crack, especially on cold mornings, and a group goes through one or two a session. A whole sleeve just adds weight and a ball rolling around the bottom of the bag.

Q: Do I need a special pickleball bag?
A: No. Any bag with a fence hook, a bottle pocket and a paddle sleeve does the job. The pickleball-specific ones earn their price with a shoe compartment and an insulated pocket, which start to matter once you play several days a week.

Q: Should I leave my pickleball bag in the car?
A: Not in summer. A hot trunk is hard on a paddle core, it ruins sunscreen, and it warms the bottle you wanted cold. Mine lives by the front door, ready for the next morning.
```

So: the five tonight, the six this week, and the last four the next time you are at the pharmacy anyway. After that, stop thinking about the bag. That is the whole point of packing it.
