This checklist is for anyone who has arrived at open play without a ball, a towel or sunscreen. Here are the 15 things to pack in your pickleball bag, sorted into three groups. Five are must-haves, six are comfort items, and four are small things you will be glad you brought. Almost everything on the list is cheap. The bag itself deserves a little thought, though. A fence hook and a shoe pocket change how the whole morning goes.

```quick
**The short answer:** paddle, two or three outdoor balls, court shoes, a water bottle and sunscreen go in first. Then a towel, a hat, sunglasses, sun sleeves, an overgrip and a snack. Finally blister pads, an instant ice pack, electrolyte packets and a light jacket. Pack it once and leave it packed.
```

## How to choose the bag itself

Start with how you get to the court. If you park beside it and carry one paddle, a sling is plenty. A sling sits across one shoulder and swings to the front so you can reach a pocket. If you walk far, or carry shoes, a jacket and two bottles, a backpack is kinder to a stiff neck.

Then look for four features. A fence hook, so the bag hangs at eye level instead of sitting on a wet court. A shoe compartment, so clean clothes stay away from the soles. An insulated pocket, which keeps a bottle cold and gives an ice pack a home. And a padded phone pocket, because a phone under a pile of balls does not survive long.

Color matters less than the zippers. Big pulls you can grab with sweaty hands are the thing you notice every time.

### Budget sling with a fence hook

This is for a first season. It also suits the player who is not sure pickleball will stick. Plenty of plain slings under about $30 hold one paddle, two balls and a bottle. Read the listing for two things. The fence hook should have stitching, not glue. The paddle sleeve should fit a paddle, since some only fit a phone.

Skip anything with a cooler pocket at this price. The insulation is thin and the pocket rarely fits a 32 oz bottle.

```buyif
+ you are in your first season and want to spend on the paddle instead.
+ you carry one paddle, two balls and a bottle.
- you need a shoe compartment or a real insulated pocket.
```

## The 15 things to pack in your pickleball bag

![Pickleball bag checklist graphic with the 15 things to pack in your pickleball bag in three columns: must-haves, comfort items and glad-I-had-it extras](/images/what-to-pack-in-your-pickleball-bag-card.png)

### The must-haves

**1. Paddle.** Still choosing? A 16 mm paddle at 7.6 to 8.0 oz with a 4.25-inch grip is the safe first buy. Our [paddle comparison](/selkirk-vs-joola-vs-crbn-paddles/) walks through it. The cheap version that is fine: a Selkirk SLK or JOOLA Essentials paddle under $100.

**2. Two or three outdoor balls.** Outdoor balls are harder, with smaller holes, and they crack in cold weather, so carry spares. Franklin X-40 is the ball at most parks, and [USA Pickleball](https://usapickleball.org/) lists it as approved. The cheap version: a six-pack of X-40s split with a friend.

**3. Court shoes.** Not running shoes. A court shoe has a flat, grippy sole and side-to-side support, which keeps an ankle from rolling. Keep them in the bag so the soles stay clean. See our shoe guides for [women](/best-pickleball-shoes-for-women/) and [men](/best-pickleball-shoes-for-men/). The ASICS Gel-Rocket line is the cheap version that is fine.

**4. Water bottle.** An insulated steel bottle, 24 to 32 oz, with a lid that will not leak in the bag. A straw lid lets you sip between points. Our [water bottle guide](/best-water-bottles-for-pickleball/) ranks the options. Any insulated 32 oz bottle with a leak-proof lid is the cheap version.

**5. Sunscreen.** SPF 30 or higher and broad spectrum. Cover the face, neck, ears, arms and the backs of the hands. Add a second coat at the two-hour mark. A stick does not leak on your paddle the way a bottle can. A drugstore mineral stick is fine. Our [pickleball skincare guide](/pickleball-skincare/) explains the rest.

### The comfort items

**6. Towel.** A small microfiber towel for your hands and the paddle handle. A sweaty grip becomes a tense grip, and a tense grip becomes a sore elbow. The cheap version: a dish-size microfiber towel from a multipack.

**7. Hat or visor.** A visor keeps sun out of your eyes and lets heat out the top. A brimmed hat covers the ears and neck too, which matters more after 50. Either one from the bin at a sporting goods store is fine; UPF 50 fabric is a bonus.

**8. Sunglasses.** Polarized lenses cut the glare off a bright court. More important, wrap-around polycarbonate lenses protect your eye from a ball at the kitchen line. Eye injuries in players over 50 are common enough that our [accessories guide](/pickleball-accessories/) covers the numbers. Cheap drugstore lenses are not impact rated.

**9. Sun sleeves.** UPF 50 arm sleeves mean no reapplying on the arms. They feel cooler than bare skin once damp and save your forearm in a dive. Any lightweight pair in your size works.

**10. Overgrip.** One spare, wrapped at home or in the car when the old one goes slick. Tourna Grip feels dry and chalky; Wilson Pro Overgrip feels tacky. A three-pack costs very little and lasts a summer.

**11. A snack.** A banana, a nut bar or a sleeve of crackers. Two hours of doubles burns more than you expect. The shaky, cranky third game is often hunger rather than age. The cheap version is whatever is already in your pantry.

### The glad-I-had-it items

**12. Small first-aid kit with blister pads.** Hydrocolloid blister pads (Band-Aid Hydro Seal or Compeed), a few regular bandages and a roll of athletic tape. The cheap version: a sandwich bag with blister pads and tape, refilled when you use one.

**13. Instant ice pack.** A squeeze-to-activate cold pack for a rolled ankle or a sore knee. Cold does the most good in the first minutes, so keep it where you can reach it. Buy a two-pack at the pharmacy and keep both in the insulated pocket.

**14. Electrolyte packets.** Plain water is fine for an hour. For a two-hour session in heat, or if you cramp at night, one packet in the second bottle helps. Liquid I.V., LMNT and Nuun tablets all work; the Nuun tube is the cheap version.

**15. Light jacket.** A packable windbreaker or a quarter-zip for the cool first game and the walk back to the car. The one you already own is the right one.

**Bonus: a paddle cover.** It protects the face from the edge of a bag and the heat of a trunk. A neoprene sleeve costs very little, and the sleeve your paddle came in works too.

## What to leave at home

Every ball you own. Outdoor balls crack, so carry three and store the rest. A full-size beach towel, a glass bottle and a hard cooler. The second paddle you never use. Lead tape and a knee brace, unless a physical therapist asked for them. And the heavy sunscreen bottle, which leaks onto your paddle face.

## How we chose

This list comes from what players over 50 reach for at park and rec courts. We cross-checked it against the specs the brands publish, not our own weigh-ins or tear-downs. We are adding our own court photos and first-hand notes as the season goes. Where a product name appears, we confirmed it exists under that name today. Where a feature was unclear, we told you to read the listing.

```faq
Q: How many pickleballs should I pack?
A: Two or three. Outdoor balls crack, especially on cold mornings, and a group goes through one or two a session. A whole sleeve just adds weight and a ball rolling around the bottom of the bag.

Q: Do I need a special pickleball bag?
A: No. Any bag with a fence hook, a bottle pocket and a paddle sleeve does the job. Pickleball-specific bags earn their price with a shoe compartment and an insulated pocket. Those matter once you play several days a week.

Q: What should I pack in my pickleball bag for a tournament?
A: The same 15 things to pack in your pickleball bag, plus a second paddle and a printed schedule. Add a folding chair and a bigger snack. Tournaments mean long waits between matches, so the comfort items matter more than at open play.

Q: Should I leave my pickleball bag in the car?
A: Not in summer heat. A hot trunk is hard on a paddle core, it ruins sunscreen, and it warms a bottle you wanted cold. Bring the bag inside and keep it by the door, ready for the next morning.
```
