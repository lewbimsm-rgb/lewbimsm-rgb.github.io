Halfway through Saturday doubles, I took stock. My hand was sliding on the handle, my ears were hot, I was squinting to find the ball, and my back had started counting every one I bent down for. None of that was the paddle or the shoes, so here are ten pickleball accessories that fix the small, repeated annoyances for not much money.

```quick
**The short version:** the ten pickleball accessories I would actually carry are a cushioned overgrip, protective sunglasses, a hat with a neck flap, UPF sun sleeves, a sling bag with a fence hook, cushioned crew socks, a ball pick-up tube, a folding chair, a paddle eraser with a cover, and an elbow strap or knee sleeve only if someone qualified suggested it. If you buy three, make them the overgrip, the glasses and the hat.
```

## Where I would start

**A cushioned overgrip** comes first, because a hand that slips grips harder, and a harder grip is where sore elbows come from. It wraps over the grip your paddle came with, soaks up sweat and adds a little padding, about a sixteenth of an inch. Tourna Grip feels dry and chalky, Wilson feels tacky, and a worn one is worse than none, so rewrap every few weeks.

Buy a three-pack and rewrap every few weeks.

**Protective sunglasses** are the one thing here I would not skip. A 2025 study in JAMA Ophthalmology counted about 1,262 pickleball eye injuries in U.S. emergency rooms in 2024, roughly seven in ten of them in people 50 or older; the [Review of Optometry summary](https://www.reviewofoptometry.com/news/article/surge-in-pickleball-correlated-with-increased-rate-of-eye-injury) has the details. The American Academy of Ophthalmology points to the ASTM F3134 standard, meaning impact-resistant polycarbonate lenses that wrap the eye; regular eyeglasses and drugstore sunglasses are not rated for impact. Polarized lenses cut glare but add nothing for impact either, so read the lens material before the color.

**A hat with a neck flap or a wide brim** replaced my cap the summer I kept coming home with red ears. A cap shades your face and nothing else, while a UPF 50 brim or flap covers the ears and the back of the neck, the two spots sunscreen misses most. The [skincare post](/pickleball-skincare/) has the rest.

**UPF 50 sun sleeves** are for the second coat of sunscreen none of us actually puts on. They feel cooler than bare skin once damp, the pricey ones block no more sun than the plain ones, and if they slide down, size down.

**A sling bag with a fence hook** keeps the bag off a wet court for anyone who walks in loaded down; a plain one under about $30 is fine if the hook is stitched rather than glued. The [packing checklist](/what-to-pack-in-your-pickleball-bag/) covers what goes inside.

I resisted **the ball pick-up tube** longest, because it looks like something a golf caddy carries, and then one drilling morning I counted the bends and gave in. You press it over a ball and the ball pops in, which turns dozens of bends into none.

**Cushioned crew socks** are for blisters and bruised heels, because cotton holds sweat and sweat is how a blister starts, while a synthetic or merino sock with cushion under the heel does not. Thorlo and Feetures both make them.

Nobody thinks they need **a folding chair** until they have stood twenty minutes between games at a court with no benches, which is harder on knees than the playing is. A low chair that packs into a tube fixes it, as long as you can stand up from it without pushing off your knees.

**A paddle eraser and a padded cover** earn a spot if your paddle has a raw carbon fiber face, because the grit fills with ball residue and a rubber eraser, used lightly, lifts it off. A fiberglass face only needs a damp cloth, and the cover protects either kind from bag zippers and hot trunks.

**An elbow strap or knee sleeve** is the one thing here I would not buy on a whim. A strap can take some load off a sore tendon and a sleeve can warm a stiff knee, but neither fixes the cause the way a lighter paddle, a looser grip and a day off do. If a joint hurts for more than two weeks, swells, or changes your swing, see a physical therapist before you see a shopping cart.

I have not worn or wrapped every one of these; each fixes one problem, costs under about $30 and comes in sizes that fit.

```faq
Q: What pickleball accessories should a beginner buy first?
A: A cushioned overgrip, because it fixes the complaint I hear most, a slipping or stinging handle, for a few dollars. Protective sunglasses come next if you play doubles, and a neck-flap hat if you play outdoors in the middle of the day.

Q: Do I need special glasses for pickleball?
A: Yes, if you want real protection. Regular eyeglasses and drugstore sunglasses are not rated for impact, so look for polycarbonate lenses that wrap the eye and a frame rated for racket sports; the American Academy of Ophthalmology points to the ASTM F3134 standard.

Q: Should I wear a knee brace or elbow strap to prevent injury?
A: Only for a named, diagnosed ache, and never as prevention. A strap or sleeve can help in that case, and it can just as easily hide a problem that needs a physical therapist. Court shoes, a warm-up and a lighter paddle prevent more injuries than any brace does.
```
