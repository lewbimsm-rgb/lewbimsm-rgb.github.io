## How much to drink

Drink about half a cup to one cup of water every 15 to 20 minutes while you are active, [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/exercise/art-20045506) says. Over two hours that is 24 to 64 ounces, more on a hot day, so a 32 oz bottle with one refill covers most of us. Thirst is not a reliable alarm at our age; it shows up late and the headache follows it home, so sip at every changeover.

Water alone is fine for an hour or in mild weather, but past two hours in real heat, or if you cramp afterward, a packet helps your body hold onto what you drink; half a packet in a 32 oz bottle keeps it from being too sweet. Packets carry sodium and some carry sugar, so ask your doctor first if you take blood pressure medicine or a diuretic.

## What to look for in the best water bottles for pickleball

Start with insulated steel, because on a 95-degree court a plastic bottle is warm by the third game, and double-wall steel keeps ice all session and shrugs off a drop. Then choose the size by your wrist as much as your thirst: forty ounces of water weighs about 2.6 pounds before the bottle, and a 64 oz is over four, so if your wrist or elbow complains, a 26 or 32 oz with a refill is the kinder choice.

The lid matters more than people expect, since your other hand is holding a paddle. Straw lids let you sip upright between points but rarely seal, so they ride upright in a side pocket; chug and flex caps seal and can lie down in a bag. Last, a carabiner through a loop hooks the bottle to the fence, and a full handle like Stanley's is easier on stiff fingers than a thin strap; every brand here says dishwasher safe, though a straw lid still wants a weekly brush.

```faq
Q: What size water bottle is best for pickleball?
A: For a two-hour outdoor session, 32 to 40 oz. Exercise guidance works out to 4 to 8 ounces every 15 to 20 minutes, so two hours lands between 24 and 64 ounces. A 32 oz bottle with one refill covers most players, and 40 oz covers a hot afternoon without a trip to the fountain.

Q: Is a straw lid or a chug lid better for pickleball?
A: A straw lid is better between points, because you sip upright without tipping your head back. A chug lid is better for a bag, because straw lids rarely seal. If you carry the bottle in a sling bag, pick the chug or flex cap and sip at the bench.

Q: Do I need electrolytes for pickleball?
A: For a mild one-hour session, water is fine. For two hours or more in heat, a packet helps your body hold onto what you drink, and the same goes if you cramp afterward. If you take blood pressure medicine or watch your sodium, ask your doctor before making packets a habit.
```
