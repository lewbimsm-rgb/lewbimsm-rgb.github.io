## Why a court shoe and not a running shoe

Pickleball is a shuffle to the side, a lunge and a hard stop, over and over. A running shoe is built to go forward, with a tall soft sole that tips when you push sideways, which is how ankles get rolled. A court shoe has a flat sole and a firm wall along the outside of the foot, and the [American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons](https://www.orthoinfo.org/staying-healthy/athletic-shoes/) says it plainly: court shoes give stability in all directions. Tennis and volleyball shoes count too.

Press the side of the shoe with your thumb and you want a firm wall, because that wall keeps your foot from sliding over the edge. Underneath you want hard rubber for outdoor courts, and under the heel you want cushion, because your knees take the stops. For men, D is the standard width, 2E is wide and 4E is extra wide, and feet that swell by the second game want at least a 2E. Try them on late in the day in your court socks, and if your heel lifts on a hard stop, keep looking.

## When to replace them

Even the best pickleball shoes for men die from the bottom up, so turn yours over once a month. Smooth tread under the ball of the foot means no grip, a heel that leans on a flat table means the foam has collapsed, and a foot that slides to the edge on a lunge means the side wall has softened. Outdoor players get roughly 60 to 80 hours per pair, about three months at two hours, three times a week; indoors, much longer.

```faq
Q: Can I play pickleball in running shoes?
A: You can, but you should not for long. Running shoes have a tall, soft, curved sole built for forward motion, and pickleball is mostly side to side, so that sole tips when you push sideways. A court shoe has a flatter sole and a firm side wall that holds your foot in place.

Q: What are the best pickleball shoes for men with wide feet?
A: Start with New Balance, which sells the 796 in D, 2E and some 4E widths and the FuelCell 996 in wide. Some ASICS Gel-Resolution versions also come in wide, and Skechers runs roomy. Nike and adidas tend to run narrow, so try those on before you order.

Q: Are pickleball shoes different from tennis shoes?
A: Hardly at all. Both have a flat outsole, a firm side wall and cushion for hard stops. Pickleball-specific shoes from Skechers, K-Swiss and HEAD tune the fit and grip for the smaller court, but a good tennis shoe from ASICS, New Balance or adidas works just as well.
```
