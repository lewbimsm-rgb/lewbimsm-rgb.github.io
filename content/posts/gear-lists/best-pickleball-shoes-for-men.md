## Why a court shoe

A running shoe's tall, soft sole is built to push you forward, and it tips when you lunge sideways. A court shoe has a flat sole, a firm wall along the outside of the foot and a wider base under the heel. The [American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons](https://www.orthoinfo.org/staying-healthy/athletic-shoes/) says it plainly: court shoes provide stability in all directions. Tennis, volleyball and pickleball-specific shoes all qualify.

## When to replace them

Court shoes die from the bottom up. Once a month, check the tread under the ball of your foot (smooth means no grip), the heel (a lean on a flat table means the foam has collapsed) and the side wall (your foot sliding to the edge on a lunge means it has softened). Outdoor players get roughly 60 to 80 hours per pair, about three months at two hours, three times a week; indoor players get much longer.

## A quick honest note

I have not worn these myself, which is probably obvious. The picks come from the makers’ sizing and width charts and from what the men at my courts say about fit. If someone in the family wears a pair, their notes go here.
```faq
Q: Can I play pickleball in running shoes?
A: You can, but not for long. Running shoes have a tall, soft, curved sole built for forward motion. Pickleball is mostly side-to-side, and that sole tips when you push sideways. A court shoe has a flatter sole and a firm side wall that holds your foot in place.

Q: What are the best pickleball shoes for men with wide feet?
A: Start with New Balance, which sells the 796 in D, 2E and some 4E widths and the FuelCell 996 in wide. Some ASICS Gel-Resolution versions also come in wide. Skechers runs roomy. Nike and adidas tend to run narrow, so try them on first.

Q: Are pickleball shoes different from tennis shoes?
A: Not by much. Both have a flat outsole, a firm side wall and cushion for hard stops. Pickleball-specific shoes from Skechers, K-Swiss and HEAD tune the fit and grip for the smaller court. A good tennis shoe from ASICS, New Balance or adidas works just as well.
```
