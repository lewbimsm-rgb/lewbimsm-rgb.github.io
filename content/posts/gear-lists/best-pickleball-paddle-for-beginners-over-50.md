## Why weight comes before everything else

Your arm swings that paddle a few hundred times in a morning, and it pays for every ounce by the third game. A heavy paddle hits harder, which sounds like a good thing until your elbow starts keeping count. Between 7.6 and 8.0 ounces is light enough to move quickly at the net and solid enough that the ball does not push it around. Much lighter than that and people start swinging harder to make up the difference, which defeats the purpose.

## Why 16 mm, and why 4.25 inches

Paddle cores come in two common thicknesses. The 16 mm core soaks up more of the ball, so shots come off softer and land where you aimed more often, and less of the sting travels up your arm. The 14 mm core is the lively one, and it can wait until you know what you want more of.

The grip matters for a similar reason. If a handle is too small you squeeze it to keep it still, and squeezing is how forearms get sore. A 4.25 inch grip fits most adult hands. When in doubt go smaller, because an overgrip will build a handle up, and nothing will shrink one.

## What I would leave alone

The $25 two-paddle sets are fine for finding out whether you like the game, and then they hold you back, because they are heavy and the ball dies on them. Long, narrow "elongated" paddles reach further but have a smaller sweet spot, which is the last thing a new player needs. And a pro model at $250 is tuned for somebody else's game.
